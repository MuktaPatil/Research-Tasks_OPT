# Module 2 - Phase B: Governance Policy

## Organizational Context

**Setting:** The Digital & Content Strategy team within a Division I
university Athletics Communications office (modeled closely on the
kind of team that would produce the Task 5/6 material this repository
is built from - season recaps, coach-analysis content, and
social-first sports storytelling for a university's athletics
department).

**Who works there:** A small team (roughly 4-8 people) of content
producers, video editors, and social media managers, reporting to a
Senior Associate AD for Communications. They are not journalists in
the traditional accountability sense - they work for the teams and
coaches they cover - but they publish under the university's
institutional name and voice, to a large public audience that includes
recruits, alumni, donors, and press.

**What they produce:** highlight packages, season-recap content,
player and coach features, social clips, game-day graphics, and
increasingly, short-form video for Instagram/TikTok/X. Budgets and
timelines are tight; the team is often producing same-day or
next-day content around live events.

**Their audience:** current and prospective students, alumni and
donors, recruits and their families, licensed media, and the general
public via social platforms - an audience that ranges from highly
engaged (season-ticket holders who'd catch an error) to a fast-scroll
social audience unlikely to read a disclosure line closely (exactly the
audience gap identified in Module 1's Context Axis reasoning).

**Where synthetic media might tempt or endanger them:** the appeal is
obvious - synthetic voice/avatar tools could let this small team
produce far more content, in more formats and languages, at a fraction
of the cost of hiring talent or scheduling real interviews. The danger
is exactly as obvious: this office speaks *as* real, named, identifiable
coaches and student-athletes to a large public audience, which is the
single highest-risk combination for the Consent Axis failure mode
described in Module 1 - and it operates under a university's
institutional credibility, which the Truth Axis reasoning suggests is
precisely the kind of borrowed authority a fabricated claim would want
to launder itself through.

## POLICY: Responsible Use of Synthetic and AI-Voiced Media

**Athletics Communications - Digital & Content Strategy**
**Effective for all content produced under the Athletics Communications
banner, across all platforms.**

### 1. Permitted Uses

Synthetic voice or video tools **may** be used for:

- **Draft/scratch narration** during internal editing - e.g. a
  synthetic voice reading a script over a rough cut so an editor can
  judge pacing before a real voiceover artist or staff member records
  the final version. Never published externally.
- **Fully disclosed, non-attributed explainer content** - e.g. a
  generic (non-cloned, non-team-affiliated) narrator voice reading
  statistical breakdowns, schedule explainers, or rules explainers,
  where no real person's likeness is depicted or implied, and an
  on-screen disclosure is present for the full duration of the clip.
- **Accessibility applications** - e.g. synthetic narration of
  already-published written recaps for screen-reader-equivalent audio
  versions, clearly labeled as machine-narrated.
- **Internal training and rehearsal material** not intended for public
  release.

In every permitted case: the content must be **true** (grounded in
verified statistics or previously-published, fact-checked material  - 
per the Truth Axis, this policy treats truthfulness as a precondition
to consider a use at all, not a mitigating factor after the fact), the
voice/likeness must be **generic or the speaker's own, with
documented consent** (see Section 3), and disclosure must be **present
in the file itself**, not only in surrounding text (per Module 1's
Context Axis finding that contextual-only disclosure does not survive
re-sharing).

### 2. Prohibited Uses - refused outright, regardless of intent

The following are refused unconditionally. Good intent, a rush
deadline, or a coach's own request does not create an exception.

- **Cloning any real coach's, student-athlete's, or staff member's
  voice or likeness to deliver any statement they did not actually
  make** - including statements the team believes the person *would*
  agree with, statements meant as parody, and statements intended for
  internal use only. There is no internal-only exception, because
  internal drafts leak (this office has, in the ordinary course of
  work, seen unpublished cuts circulate informally).
- **Any synthetic depiction of an opposing team's coaches, athletes, or
  staff**, for any purpose, including rivalry content, hype videos, or
  "reaction" content, even when clearly framed as a joke.
- **Any synthetic content that a reasonable viewer could mistake for a
  live, real-time statement** - e.g. a synthetic "breaking" injury
  announcement, recruiting decision, or coaching-change statement, even
  if planned for later correction.
- **Any use during an active NCAA compliance matter, personnel
  investigation, or litigation involving the university's athletics
  program**, regardless of whether the specific content would otherwise
  be permitted.
- **Removing, shortening, or visually minimizing a required disclosure**
  to make content "cleaner," including at a coach's or administrator's
  request.

### 3. Consent Workflow

When a real person's voice, likeness, or identifiable characteristics
are used in any synthetic capacity (including the permitted "own voice"
case in Section 1):

- **Who obtains consent:** the Senior Associate AD for Communications
  or their designated deputy - never the content producer alone, to
  avoid a junior staffer feeling pressure to secure informal consent
  under deadline.
- **What is documented:** a signed, dated consent form specifying
  exactly what will be produced (script or script summary, intended
  use, intended platforms, and expiration), kept on file with the
  athletics compliance office.
- **How long it is valid:** consent is granted **per project**, not as
  a standing blanket approval - a coach who consents to one synthetic
  explainer clip has not thereby consented to future ones.
- **How it is revoked:** the person may revoke consent at any time,
  including after publication; revocation triggers the takedown
  procedure in Section 7 within 24 hours of the request.

### 4. Disclosure Standards

- Every synthetic-media artifact carries an **on-screen text label**
  present for its full duration ("AI-GENERATED CONTENT"), not just at
  the start, since clips are frequently trimmed or excerpted downstream.
- Audio-only content carries an **equivalent spoken disclosure** at the
  start of the clip, in addition to any text metadata, because spoken
  disclosure is harder to strip during re-encoding than text alone.
- The office's **publishing checklist** (referenced from the standard
  production workflow) requires confirming disclosure is visible
  *in the exported file itself*, not only in the caption or post text  - 
  directly responding to Module 1's finding that caption-only
  disclosure does not survive re-sharing.
- Any staff member re-sharing or excerpting synthetic content produced
  under this policy is responsible for preserving the disclosure in the
  new post; automated posting tools used by this office must be
  configured to reject uploads where the disclosure overlay has been
  cropped out.

### 5. Provenance Requirements

- Where the generating tool supports embedded content credentials
  (e.g. C2PA), they must be enabled and left intact through every
  editing and export step.
- Where the generating tool does not support this (as was true of both
  free-tier tools used in Task 6), the office maintains its own internal
  provenance log: tool name and version, date generated, script used,
  and staff member responsible, retained for no less than three years.
- Raw, unedited synthetic outputs are archived internally even when
  the published version is trimmed, so the full original context can
  be produced if a dispute arises later.

### 6. Review and Approval

- Every proposed synthetic-media project requires **written approval
  from the Senior Associate AD for Communications before production
  begins** - not just before publication. This catches concept-level
  problems before staff time is spent, and prevents the common failure
  mode of a nearly-finished piece being approved on inertia.
- Approval applies the standard in this policy directly: does the
  proposed use fall under Section 1, does it touch any Section 2
  prohibition, and is a Section 3 consent process required and
  completed.
- Any proposal touching a real, named person's likeness additionally
  requires sign-off from that person's own department (coaching staff,
  student-athlete via compliance office) independent of the
  Communications office's own approval.

### 7. Incident Response

If synthetic content produced under this policy is misused,
misattributed, taken out of context, or reaches an unintended audience:

1. **Immediate takedown request** to the platform(s) involved, filed
   within 24 hours of discovery.
2. **Direct notification** to any real person whose likeness or voice
   was involved, regardless of whether they were the one affected by
   the misuse.
3. **Public correction** issued from the same account and in a
   comparably prominent format as the original post - not buried in a
   reply or a since-expired story.
4. **Internal incident review** within one week, documenting what
   happened, whether this policy's controls were followed, and whether
   the policy itself needs revision.
5. **Reporting to university legal counsel** for any incident involving
   an unauthorized third party's reuse of university-produced synthetic
   content, or any incident involving a non-consensual likeness.

### 8. Refusal

This office declines to produce synthetic media at all, even for an
otherwise-permitted use under Section 1, when:

- **The requester cannot articulate why real recorded material (an
  actual interview, an actual voiceover session) would not serve the
  same purpose.** Synthetic media is a supplement to real production
  capacity, not a default substitute for it; if a real recording is
  feasible on the timeline, this office will not reach for a synthetic
  shortcut merely for convenience.
- **The request originates from, or is intended primarily to serve, a
  fundraising, political, or reputation-management purpose** rather
  than a factual, athletic-program purpose - this office produces
  sports content, not persuasive advocacy content, and synthetic media's
  persuasive-authority effect (Module 1's Truth Axis) is a real risk
  specifically in donor- or reputation-facing contexts.
- **Staff capacity does not allow for the full review and consent
  workflow above to be completed properly** - a rushed synthetic-media
  approval is treated as equivalent to no approval at all, and the
  office defaults to real production or delay rather than skip steps.
